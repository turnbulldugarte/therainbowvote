# The Rainbow Vote: Website Critique and Pre-Launch Marketing Strategy

**Report date:** 4 October 2026  
**Publication date:** 2 February 2027  
**Runway:** 121 days  
**Method:** Dual-agent assessment (independent design/strategy review plus deterministic code/evidence review), synthesized against the current React site.

## Executive verdict

The site is visually distinctive, credible, and unusually well aligned with the subject of the book. Its campaign-poster typography, ballot imagery, hand-drawn data motifs, bold colour system, interactive book hero, research statistics, chapter summaries, and endorsements form a coherent identity that could not be transferred unchanged to another book.

The central weakness is not visual design. It is marketing architecture. The site currently behaves as a polished academic-book brochure:

> Attention → evidence → external retailer

It needs to become a launch platform:

> Attention → audience relevance → low-friction capture → nurture → preorder → advocacy

At present, visitors who are not ready to preorder leave anonymously. Journalists, academics, LGBTQ+ readers, event organisers, booksellers, and political professionals all receive essentially the same journey. The site also lacks the assets and pathways needed to support publicity, events, course adoption, partner sharing, and launch-week activation.

The highest-return decision is therefore to keep the existing visual world, but build an owned-audience and content system around it.

## Strategic objective

Use the website as the central hub for a four-month campaign that does three things:

1. Converts high-intent visitors into tracked preorder clicks.
2. Captures interested visitors who are not ready to purchase and nurtures them toward launch.
3. Gives journalists, academics, organisers, booksellers, and partners everything they need to amplify the book.

The campaign should position *The Rainbow Vote* as both:

- a major evidence-led intervention about an overlooked electorate; and
- a timely, human account of how identity, rights, representation, and lived experience shape democratic behaviour.

That second half matters. The current phrase “how to win their ballot” is sharp and memorable for political and media audiences, but can make LGBTQ+ readers feel studied as an electoral object. The surrounding copy should consistently balance electoral power with agency, representation, democratic stakes, and lived experience.

## What is already working

### 1. A highly specific visual identity

The marigold-and-ink palette, Anton/Caveat/Inter type system, ballot cube, campaign-wall cards, hard shadows, hand-drawn charts, and interactive book-opening hero feel authored for this book. This is the strongest part of the site and should be preserved.

### 2. Evidence-led persuasion

The 25,000+ respondents, 4,000+ LGBTQ+ respondents, original experiments, chapter structure, press credentials, and endorsements create authority without relying on empty hype. That tone suits academic, political, and media audiences.

### 3. Clear preorder visibility

The preorder action is repeated in the hero, navigation, mid-page section, chapter page, author page, and footer. Gay’s the Word/Bookshop.org is given a meaningful ethical differentiator while Amazon and Princeton University Press remain available.

### 4. Good accessibility intent

The code includes a skip link, clear focus treatments, semantic structure, reduced-motion handling, image dimensions, and descriptive accessible labels for retailer links.

## Core gaps, in priority order

### P0 — No owned-audience conversion

All meaningful CTAs send people to third-party retailers. Visitors who are interested but not ready to preorder cannot remain connected to the campaign.

**Add:**

- An email signup with a real value exchange: sample chapter, short findings briefing, launch invitation, or “five findings from 25,000 voters.”
- A compact signup module near the first major proof section and a second at the end of the page.
- Consent and privacy language appropriate to the email platform.
- Segmentation by interest: reader, academic/educator, media, events/politics.
- A short welcome sequence rather than an undifferentiated newsletter.

**Recommended promise:** “Get a chapter preview, new findings, and launch-event invitations.”

### P1 — One message is serving too many audiences

The site has at least five plausible audiences:

| Audience | What they need to hear | Best next action |
|---|---|---|
| LGBTQ+ and politically engaged readers | This book explains lived political experience, representation, rights, and electoral power. | Read an excerpt; preorder; join the list. |
| Journalists and producers | There are timely, data-rich stories and an authoritative, interview-ready expert. | View news hooks; download media kit; request interview/review copy. |
| Academics and students | This is a major comparative contribution with original data and methods. | View contents/sample; course adoption; request desk/review copy. |
| Parties, campaigners, and civil society | The research reveals how support is earned, mobilised, and lost. | Download briefing; book a talk; preorder/bulk order. |
| Booksellers, libraries, and event organisers | The book has a clear audience, credible author, and event potential. | Request event; order information; access promotional assets. |

**Add:** a concise “Who this book is for” section with three primary pathways rather than five equally weighted choices. Recommended top-level grouping:

- Readers
- Research and teaching
- Media, events, and practitioners

### P1 — No launch or press hub

The site should become the definitive source of current launch information.

**Add a Launch / Press page containing:**

- Publication date, publisher, ISBN, formats, territories, and retailer links.
- A one-paragraph synopsis and a 50-word short description.
- Downloadable high-resolution cover, author photographs, and author bios in short and long forms.
- Three to five media-ready findings or story angles.
- Review-copy and interview contact.
- Events calendar with RSVP links and recordings after each event.
- Speaking, podcast, panel, and bookstore-event enquiries.
- Course adoption, desk-copy, bulk-order, library-request, and bookseller information.

### P1 — No content engine or sharing loop

The book has unusually strong campaign material—data, comparative findings, experiments, and provocative chapter questions—but it is presented as static summary copy.

Create a repeatable findings-led content series. Each item should contain one idea, one chart or visual, one implication, one shareable line, and one CTA.

Recommended recurring formats:

- **The Rainbow Vote finding of the week:** one data point or counterintuitive result.
- **Inside the research:** short explanations of the surveys and experiments.
- **Chapter preview:** one question and one takeaway from each chapter.
- **What this means now:** commentary connecting the research to relevant political moments.
- **In conversation:** short interviews, podcast clips, event excerpts, and endorsements.
- **Myth / evidence:** a claim about LGBTQ+ voters contrasted with the evidence.

Every asset should lead to one of three outcomes: email signup, event RSVP, or preorder.

### P1 — Weak publicity and partner enablement

Earned reach will depend on other people being able to communicate the book quickly and accurately.

**Add:**

- A partner toolkit with square, portrait, and landscape social cards.
- Suggested post copy and tracked links.
- A one-page book briefing for LGBTQ+ organisations, research centres, universities, and political groups.
- A launch-event pack for bookshops, departments, and community organisations.
- A simple share action after signup and, where possible, after an outbound preorder click.

### P2 — Thin measurement and retailer experience

GoatCounter records routes, but the source does not show explicit tracking for outbound preorder clicks, email conversions, sample downloads, media enquiries, event RSVPs, or campaign sources.

**Add:**

- Events for every retailer click, including page position and retailer.
- Events for signup completion, sample download, media enquiry, and event RSVP.
- A consistent UTM taxonomy for email, press, partners, organic social, paid social, podcast, and event campaigns.
- Campaign-specific landing URLs for major partners and media appearances.
- A lightweight weekly dashboard.
- Publication date and retailer context beside the first preorder CTA.

Suggested primary KPI: **qualified preorder clicks**.  
Suggested secondary KPIs: **email conversion rate, sample downloads, event RSVPs, media enquiries, partner traffic, and retailer click-through by source**.

### P2 — Homepage proof is strong but poorly staged

The opening creates energy, the statistics build authority, and the chapters build interest. Momentum then flattens through long author and endorsement content. Six substantial endorsements create depth but reduce scanability.

**Improve by:**

- Showing the publication date and a short reader promise in the hero.
- Shortening the opening lede.
- Leading with two or three concise endorsements and revealing the rest on demand.
- Replacing some long-form proof with quotable findings and outcomes.
- Ending with anticipation and belonging—signup, launch event, sample, and share—not only legal/footer content.

### P2 — Performance and technical campaign readiness

The 5.3 MB hero video uses `preload="auto"`, which can consume bandwidth before the primary CTA becomes usable on mobile. Load the poster first and defer or reduce video loading. The mobile menu animates `max-height`, which causes avoidable layout work, though this is low impact.

The site also needs:

- Route-specific titles and social metadata.
- Absolute Open Graph image URLs in production.
- `Book` structured data/JSON-LD.
- A sitemap and deliberate search-indexing checks.
- A visible privacy/contact route before collecting email.
- Removal of unnecessary keyboard stops on non-interactive endorsement cards.

The automated detector reported only two warnings: the mobile navigation’s `max-height` transition and a rounded chapter-card accent border. The latter appears intentional and is likely a false positive within the established campaign-poster style.

## Recommended site architecture

Keep the current Home, Chapters, and Author pages, then add:

1. **Insights** — index of findings, chapter previews, commentary, and press appearances.
2. **Launch / Events** — upcoming events, RSVP links, recordings, and launch-week schedule.
3. **Press / Media** — media kit, story angles, review-copy request, interview contact, assets.
4. **Resources** — sample chapter, teaching/course adoption, briefings, library and bulk-order routes.

The homepage should use the following order:

1. Title, reader promise, publication date, primary preorder CTA, and email/sample CTA.
2. Three audience-relevance statements.
3. The most surprising finding or signature chart.
4. Research scale and credibility statistics.
5. Selected chapter hooks.
6. Two or three short endorsements.
7. Author authority and selected linked coverage.
8. Upcoming event or newest insight.
9. Final signup, preorder, and share actions.

## 121-day roadmap

### Phase 1: Foundation — 4 October to 1 November

**Goal:** stop losing interested visitors and make all traffic measurable.

- Choose and configure the email platform.
- Create the lead magnet: preferably a sample chapter plus a concise findings briefing.
- Add signup modules, consent copy, privacy page, and welcome sequence.
- Add publication date and secondary email/sample CTA above the fold.
- Implement outbound click and microconversion tracking.
- Define the UTM taxonomy and weekly dashboard.
- Build the Press / Media page and assemble downloadable assets.
- Add audience pathways and revise the most instrumental framing.
- Defer/optimise the hero video and add route-specific metadata plus Book JSON-LD.

**Exit condition:** every visitor can buy, subscribe, download, enquire, or RSVP—and each action is measurable.

### Phase 2: Audience building — 2 November to 13 December

**Goal:** establish a repeatable reason to visit and subscribe.

- Begin a weekly finding/chapter-preview series.
- Publish the sample chapter or excerpt.
- Create the partner toolkit and recruit aligned organisations, research centres, departments, newsletters, and bookshops.
- Begin review-copy, podcast, newsletter, and long-lead media outreach.
- Announce initial events and open RSVPs.
- Build course-adoption, bulk-order, and library-request pathways.
- Test two or three homepage/email messages by campaign source rather than redesigning the visual identity.

**Exit condition:** the campaign has a growing owned list, a content rhythm, confirmed partners, and a forward event calendar.

### Phase 3: Anticipation — 14 December to 10 January

**Goal:** maintain momentum through the holiday period without exhausting the audience.

- Publish evergreen “best of the research” content at a lighter cadence.
- Prepare January assets, scheduling, tracking links, launch emails, and press follow-ups.
- Collect early reactions, coverage, event confirmations, and new endorsements.
- Produce reusable chart cards and short vertical-video scripts.
- Confirm the launch-week run of show and retailer availability.

**Exit condition:** January content and launch operations are ready before attention returns after the holiday period.

### Phase 4: Conversion push — 11 January to 1 February

**Goal:** turn accumulated attention into preorders, RSVPs, and coverage.

- Publish one major finding or chapter reveal each week.
- Make the publication date, preorder, launch event, and sample highly prominent.
- Run author Q&As, podcast appearances, partner takeovers, and short video explainers.
- Update the site continuously with coverage and confirmed events.
- Send segmented email messages based on reader, academic, media, and event interests.
- Use paid promotion only for content/audiences already demonstrating organic conversion.
- Prepare purchaser sharing and review prompts.

**Exit condition:** the full audience knows exactly when the book launches, why it matters, and what to do next.

### Phase 5: Launch week — 2 to 8 February

**Goal:** concentrate social proof, availability, events, and participation.

- Switch “preorder” language to “buy now” everywhere on launch day.
- Feature live retailer links, events, press, interviews, and launch-day media.
- Send coordinated launch emails to each segment.
- Activate partner toolkits and track results by partner/source.
- Invite purchasers to share, request the book at a library, recommend it to a course, and leave retailer reviews when eligible.
- Capture recordings and convert launch activity into evergreen assets.

### Phase 6: Post-launch — from 9 February

**Goal:** turn launch attention into a durable readership and adoption funnel.

- Request reviews at an appropriate interval.
- Publish event recordings and media highlights.
- Release reading-group, teaching, and discussion resources.
- Continue topical commentary linked to the book’s findings.
- Develop the course-adoption, library, event, and paperback lifecycle.

## Minimum viable launch stack

If time is constrained, ship these seven elements first:

1. Email capture plus a sample/briefing.
2. A three-email welcome sequence.
3. Publication date and dual CTA in the hero.
4. Press/media kit and contact route.
5. Events page with RSVP capability.
6. Tracked retailer, signup, download, and RSVP events.
7. A weekly finding-led content series with reusable social assets.

Everything else is secondary to this stack.

## Suggested welcome sequence

1. **Immediately:** deliver the sample and explain the core argument in one paragraph.
2. **After 3 days:** share the most counterintuitive finding and how the evidence was collected.
3. **After 7 days:** present three audience-relevant reasons to read, the publication date, events, and preorder options.

After that, subscribers should enter the normal findings/events sequence with frequency expectations stated clearly.

## Personas and failure points

### Jordan — politically engaged LGBTQ+ reader

The hero is compelling, but dense political-science copy and “how to win their ballot” can suggest the site is speaking about Jordan to political professionals. There is no excerpt, reader-centred promise, or lower-commitment signup. Jordan may admire the site and leave.

### Priya — journalist or podcast producer

The research scale and press logos establish credibility, but Priya cannot immediately find story hooks, a press kit, downloadable images, review-copy information, or an interview contact. The site creates interest without enabling coverage.

### Alex — lecturer or researcher

The chapters and endorsements build confidence, but there is no table-of-contents download, sample chapter, citation metadata, course-adoption information, or desk-copy route. Academic interest has nowhere to convert except a retail preorder.

## Important implementation details

- Show “Publishes 2 February 2027” beside the first preorder action.
- Offer Bookshop.org/Gay’s the Word as the recommended route, with other retailers clearly secondary.
- Use audience-specific landing pages or query-tagged modules for major press, podcast, partner, and academic campaigns.
- Link selected press logos to actual appearances where context and rights allow.
- Avoid putting five equal choices in one section; use three top-level audience paths.
- Keep forms short: email required; interest optional; name optional.
- Do not gate every asset. Gate the most valuable launch briefing/sample, while keeping press facts and basic academic metadata open.
- Treat email signups and event RSVPs as conversions, not merely preorder clicks.

## Questions requiring a strategic decision

1. **Primary audience:** Should the first screen address LGBTQ+ and politically engaged readers first, or retain political/academic professionals as the lead audience?
2. **Lead magnet:** Is a sample chapter available, or should the initial offer be a designed “five findings from 25,000 voters” briefing?
3. **Campaign scope:** Should the first implementation focus on the seven-item minimum stack, or build the full launch/press/resources architecture immediately?

## Bottom line

The site does not need a new identity. It needs a campaign operating system. Preserve the current design, add an owned audience, divide the message by visitor intent, turn the book’s evidence into a serial content programme, equip media and partners to amplify it, and instrument every meaningful action. Those changes will make the site useful every week between now and 2 February—not just at the moment someone is already ready to preorder.
